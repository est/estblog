The demoscene is a computer art subculture that specializes in producing demos, which are non-interactive audio-visual presentations run real-time on a computer. The main goal of a demo is to show off programming, artistic and musical skills.

The demoscene first appeared during the 8-bit era on computers such as the Commodore 64 and ZX Spectrum, and came to prominence during the rise of the 16/32-bit home computers (the Atari ST and the Amiga). In the early years, demos had a strong connection with software cracking. When a cracked program was started, the cracker or his team would take credit with a graphical introduction called a "crack intro" (shortened cracktro). Later, the making of intros and standalone demos evolved into a new subculture independent of the software piracy scene.

You would be right in thinking that the demoscene PC wise is heavily skewed towards DirectX. In fact, you are probably aware of the 4k/64k productions of late being very impressive, but that's due in part to the functionality made in cutting edge DirectX releases.

It is a curious issue however. If you go to any demo party you will find a significant number of attendants using Linux based systems. Well, that and AmigaOS of course!

Paniq of Farb-Rausch is probably the most passionate Linux based demoscener, and he has set up the site linuxdemos.org - which is the main place for you linux demoscene fix. Bear in mind also, pouet.net does have the occasional multiplatform release too.

The attitude towards linux is mixed really. Some demosceners just aren't interested in the political/freedom side of things, and are happy to use Windows as they find it does what they require (whether you agree with that or not). Some advanced sceners say they have tried linux but just struggle to live without Visual Studio's IDE or the DirectX API which are both very mature, and often the demosceners use them as part of thier own jobs.

As with Linux generally, I suppose it will be a growth area, and it's probably a matter of time until more and more demos come out.

Until then, it's Wine for most things!
